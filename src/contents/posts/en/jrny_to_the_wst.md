Actually I should have published this blog one and a half months earlier, but the fact is that I only set up my blog last week, so it feels a bit absurd to be sharing my traveling experience nearly two months after.

I have to admit that it **was** a wise choice to choose this **tremendous** university, which offered me such a **tremendous** opportunity to embrace the American culture at UW-Madison.

Well, to be honest, I wasn't as excited about the coming overseas study experience as you might imagine — at least earlier this year, but in the end I ~~was ordered to~~ appreciatively seize the chance though it cost me an arm and a leg.

Thankfully under the **tremendous** leadership of BPE (Best President Ever) Donald J. Trump, I paid a \$350 SEVIS fee (Protection Racket) to ICE, and a \$185 visa application fee to the U.S. Consulate.

> **Welcome to the Land of the Free. Please Pay First.**

The **tremendous** country — the United States of America — has made me a more devoted Mao's Warrior than ever before.

![](/images/posts/jrny_to_the_wst/mao.png)

## Pudong International Airport (PVG) and Airbus A380

People fear the unknown, and I was unwilling to travel 7,500 miles to the other end of the earth on my own. Thankfully my roommate was taking the same flight, so I had someone to chat with.

Getting up early in the morning, my parents drove me to Shanghai Pudong International Airport (上海浦东国际机场).

For this airport, comments like these are common:

> PVG is **SOOOOOO** big that you need to take underground inside.

> PVG is bigger than Macau.

> Arrive 2 hr prior to departure and you may still have to run in the terminal. (But I arrived 10 hours before departure lol.)

But the thing is, I felt the size of Terminal 2's Passenger Waiting Area even slightly smaller than that of Hangzhou East Railway Station (杭州火车东站), while T2 is the largest among all three terminals.

The check-in counter was very efficient: my baggage drop-off was handled quickly, and my boarding pass was printed in an instant.

![](/images/posts/jrny_to_the_wst/boarding_pass.jpg)

And the Customs Officer was trying his best to be thorough: he kindly requested my Form I-20, questioned whether I was an undergraduate or a doctoral student, how long I would stay, and warned me again about the risks of going abroad. Then I noticed a group of girls heading to Kuala Lumpur were also intercepted by him, and were requested to show their hotel booking details. By contrast, the U.S. officer just asked for essential documents and let me go.

By the boarding gate I spotted the Airbus A380 I would be flying on.

![](/images/posts/jrny_to_the_wst/a380.jpg)

My very first time flying was on an A380 — I’m certainly a lucky person, at least before having settled down at my economy class seat. Its larger size did not result in more legroom, but rather more seats. In the end it **is** the largest airliner ever, isn't it?

Disheartening fact: This flight took off at midnight and landed before sunrise, so I didn't have a chance to take a good look at it.

![](/images/posts/jrny_to_the_wst/a380_sfo.jpg)
*A380 at SFO shot by [Mike Ogilvy](https://mikeogilvy.github.io/photography/)*

That was my first time on an airplane, and also my first time abroad.

The flight was boring.

Actually America isn't really part of what people mean by "the West", but both of my flights were heading west, so just let me call it a journey to the **West**.

For an **international** airport, DXB doesn't have a strong Muslim atmosphere. It is just a modern airport, with busy passengers and everything in apple-pie order.

## Chicago and Madison

After a boring 14-hour flight, I managed to land at Chicago O'Hare International Airport (ORD). Unfortunately, landing was apparently not the end of the journey. The plane then proceeded to taxi around O'Hare for what felt like an eternity, taking a rather scenic tour of nearly half the airport. At this point, I was beginning to wonder whether we had landed in Chicago or had simply booked ourselves on a sightseeing tour of its runways.

Apparently, this was partly thanks to O'Hare's rather questionable airport layout — a masterpiece of infrastructure planning that seems to have been assembled over decades from accumulated patches rather than any coherent design.

But I digress. Back to the actual business of entering the country: clearing immigration. Fortunately, this turned out to be surprisingly straightforward. The officer asked me just one or two questions, gave my documents a quick look, and sent me on my way. No drama, no lengthy interrogation — ICE was apparently feeling rather generous that day, and decided to give me a pass with barely any fuss.

> Thanks for the invite. Currently in the U.S., having just landed. (泻药。人在美国，刚下飞机。)

After going 40 hours without a bed, we decided to rest near O'Hare for one night before heading to Madison. There we had our first meal in the U.S.: McDonald's.

Then I had a long(?) sleep.

![](/images/posts/jrny_to_the_wst/dlg_en.png)

Then I had breakfast at the hotel.

![](/images/posts/jrny_to_the_wst/breakfast.jpg)

Almost everything seemed to come with an unnecessarily generous amount of sugar. I genuinely wondered whether sugar was still a novelty in this country. It was almost as if Americans had only recently discovered it, and were now determined to put it in absolutely everything.

Then, *la dernière partie de mon voyage*: taking the coach to Madison. Apparently, the U.S. has a well-developed bus system; I could easily travel to my final destination.

![](/images/posts/jrny_to_the_wst/coach_usa.jpg)

![](/images/posts/jrny_to_the_wst/wt_coach.jpg)

I've spotted it tens of thousands of times on several U.S. maps in *War Thunder*.

And with that, this coach carried me on the final leg of my journey, my journey to the **West**.
